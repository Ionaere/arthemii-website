Architecture

All the works in this section are generated with artificial intelligence, but each of them begins with a drawing by hand. First Arthemii makes a drawing on paper — many of them can be seen in the Drawings section. The architecture is then generated from that original drawing, without any request to distort it. This is possible because the drawing is, in essence, graphics: a clear schematic through which the architecture is visualised.

How it is made

From drawing to building

Every building in this section begins as a free line drawing on paper. The line is drawn intuitively, without a preliminary sketch, plan or brief, and the finished drawing is a clear graphic: a schematic of curves, ribbons and enclosed spaces. That graphic is the score from which the architecture is generated.

  1. 01

    Free line drawing

    Each concept begins by hand, on paper. The line is drawn intuitively, without a preliminary sketch, plan or brief.

  2. 02

    Turned and read as a structure

    The drawing is turned until it stands as a building and is read as a structure: lines become ribbons and frames, closed shapes become rooms, terraces and voids. The drawing itself is not changed.

  3. 03

    Render

    The drawing is then visualised as architecture with generative AI, with no request to distort it. The software gives the lines scale, material, light and context; the form itself comes from the hand drawing.

Principles of the method

  • The hand stays the author

    The form is decided on paper. The software visualises the drawing; it does not invent the building from a text prompt.

  • No distortion

    The renders keep the proportions and curves of the drawing; nothing is stretched or redrawn to fit a building.

  • Turning, not redrawing

    A drawing can be read upright, upside down or on its side. Turning it by 90°, 180° or 270° is the only change made before it becomes architecture.

  • One drawing, many buildings

    The same drawing, turned differently or placed in another setting, can produce several distinct buildings.

  • From stained glass to architecture

    The drawings share their language with the stained glass works: lines that frame light. In the buildings they become ribbons of structure with light and greenery between them.

Free Line Architecture is a research series rather than commissioned buildings. It asks how the freedom of a drawn line could inform future facades, public structures and light-filled spaces.
